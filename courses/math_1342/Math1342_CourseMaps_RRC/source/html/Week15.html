Objectives		 Learning Materials#Activities#Assessments#
  1. Find and use theoretical probabilities from normal, t, chi-squared and F distributions to form confidence intervals and test hypotheses. Apply the 95% rule to normal and to approximately normal distributions.
  2. Analyze relationships between two quantitative variables using correlation and linear regression.
  3. Demonstrate skills in inference for regression and ANOVA techniques.
  1. Identify when ANOVA is appropriate and state H₀ and Hₐ in symbols and words. (CO12)
  2. Explain what SSG and SSE measure and how the ratio of between-group to within-group variability gives evidence against H₀. (CO12)
  3. Complete an ANOVA table (df, MS, F) from given sums of squares, interpret the F statistic and p-value, and check the conditions. (CO4, CO12)
  4. State the simple linear model, distinguish the population slope β₁ and correlation ρ from the sample estimates b₁ and r, and interpret the slope in context. (CO5, CO12)
  5. Use regression output to test H₀: β₁ = 0 (equivalently ρ = 0) with a t statistic on n − 2 degrees of freedom, state the conclusion in context, and explain why the slope and correlation tests give the same p-value. (CO12)
  6. Construct and interpret a confidence interval for the slope, interpret R² as the proportion of variability explained, and check the conditions with a residual plot. (CO12)

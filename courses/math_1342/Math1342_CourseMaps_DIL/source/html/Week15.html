  1. Find and use theoretical probabilities from normal, t, chi-squared and F distributions to form confidence intervals and test hypotheses. Apply the 95% rule to normal and to approximately normal distributions.
  2. Apply ideas of appropriate sampling techniques and experimental design to data production.
  3. Demonstrate skills in hypothesis testing for means and proportions, for single populations and comparison of two populations.
  4. Demonstrate skills in hypothesis testing using chi-squared tests.
  5. Demonstrate skills in inference for regression and ANOVA techniques.
  1. Identify when ANOVA is appropriate and state H₀ and Hₐ in symbols and words. (CO12)
  2. Explain what SSG and SSE measure and how the ratio of between-group to within-group variability gives evidence against H₀. (CO12)
  3. Complete an ANOVA table (df, MS, F) from given sums of squares, interpret the F statistic and p-value, and check the conditions. (CO4, CO12)
  4. Choose the correct test among one- and two-proportion z, one- and two-sample t, paired t, and chi-square, and carry out a complete six-step hypothesis test. (CO10, CO11)
  5. Explain what the study design (random sampling vs. random assignment) allows a conclusion to claim, and distinguish statistical from practical significance. (CO7, CO10)

  1. Find and use empirical probabilities in bootstrap distributions to find confidence intervals and in randomization distributions to test hypotheses.
  2. Estimate single means, difference of two means, single proportions and difference of two proportions using confidence intervals. Interpret the results.
  3. Demonstrate skills in hypothesis testing for means and proportions, for single populations and comparison of two populations.
  1. Describe Type I and Type II errors in the context of a study, relate α to the probability of a Type I error, and explain the trade-off between the two errors and the effect of sample size. (CO10)
  2. Explain how multiple testing and publication bias produce false positives, and distinguish statistical significance from practical significance. (CO10)
  3. Describe how to generate randomization samples for a given study design (coin flips, re-randomizing groups, shifting data then bootstrapping). (CO3)
  4. Compare bootstrap and randomization distributions by their center and use, identifying each center for given data. (CO3)
  5. Use a 95% confidence interval to reach a two-tailed test conclusion at α = 0.05, and decide whether a confidence interval or a hypothesis test fits a research question. (CO9, CO10)

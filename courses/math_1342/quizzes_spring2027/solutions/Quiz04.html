Problem 1 [3 pts]

A survey of 400 ACC students recorded whether each student has math anxiety (A) and whether they took a developmental math course (D).

Math anxietyNo math anxietyTotal
Took developmental math5466120
Did not84196280
Total138262400

Find each probability for a randomly selected student. (0.5 pt each)

  1. \(P(A)\)
  2. \(P(A \text{ and } D)\)
  3. \(P(A \text{ or } D)\)
  4. \(P(A \mid D)\)
  5. \(P(D \mid A)\)
  6. Are math anxiety and taking developmental math independent? Justify with your probabilities.

(a) \(138/400 = 0.345\). (b) \(54/400 = 0.135\). (c) \(P(A)+P(D)-P(A \text{ and } D) = (138+120-54)/400 = 204/400 = 0.51\). (d) \(54/120 = 0.45\). (e) \(54/138 \approx 0.391\). (f) Not independent: \(P(A \mid D) = 0.45 \ne P(A) = 0.345\).

Problem 3 [3 pts]

A random sample of ACC students gives a 95% confidence interval of \((18.2,\ 23.8)\) for the mean number of hours worked per week. (1 pt each)

  1. Find the point estimate and the margin of error.
  2. Interpret the interval in context.
  3. A classmate says, “95% of ACC students work between 18.2 and 23.8 hours a week.” Explain what is wrong with this statement.

(a) Point estimate \(= (18.2+23.8)/2 = 21.0\) hours; margin of error \(= 2.8\) hours.

(b) We are 95% confident that the mean number of hours worked per week by all ACC students is between 18.2 and 23.8 hours.

(c) The interval estimates the population mean, not where individual students fall. Individual students vary far more widely than the interval.

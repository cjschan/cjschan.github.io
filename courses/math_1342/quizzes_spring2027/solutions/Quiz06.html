Quiz 6

Hypothesis Tests, P-Values, and Significance

Worked Solutions

Covers Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3

Instructions. This is a take-home quiz. You may use your lecture notes and online resources, but the work you submit must be your own. Show your work clearly to receive credit.

StatKey problems. Problems marked STATKEY are done in StatKey (lock5stat.com/StatKey). Let D = the last digit of your ACC student ID, and use the row of each table that matches your D. For every StatKey problem, upload a screenshot of your own StatKey run that shows the graph and the summary panel (number of samples, mean, standard error, and any tail or percentile values you used). The numbers you report must match your screenshot. Answers that do not match your D or your screenshot receive no credit.

Total: 10 points

Problem 1 [2 pts]

State the null and alternative hypotheses for each scenario, and define any parameter you use. (1 pt each)

  1. A college wants evidence that fewer than 30% of its students use the campus tutoring center.
  2. A researcher wants to know whether the mean resting heart rate is different for athletes and non-athletes.

(a) \(H_0: p = 0.30\), \(H_a: p < 0.30\), where \(p\) is the proportion of all students who use the tutoring center.

(b) \(H_0: \mu_A = \mu_N\), \(H_a: \mu_A \ne \mu_N\), where \(\mu_A\) and \(\mu_N\) are the mean resting heart rates of all athletes and all non-athletes.

Problem 2 [2 pts]

A test of \(H_0: p = 0.5\) vs. \(H_a: p > 0.5\) for the proportion of voters who support a bond gives a p-value of 0.031. (1 pt each)

  1. Interpret this p-value in context.
  2. What is the decision at \(\alpha = 0.05\)? At \(\alpha = 0.01\)?

(a) If exactly half of all voters supported the bond, there would be only a 3.1% chance of getting a sample proportion as large as (or larger than) ours just by random chance.

(b) At \(\alpha = 0.05\): reject \(H_0\) (0.031 < 0.05). At \(\alpha = 0.01\): do not reject \(H_0\) (0.031 > 0.01).

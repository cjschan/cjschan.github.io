Review from Section 2.2 — Measures of Spread

Standard Deviation

A measure of how spread out the data are around the mean.

Formula

\[ s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} \]
Sample standard deviation: \(s\). Population standard deviation: \(\sigma\).

Interpretation

  • Gives a rough estimate of the typical distance from the mean.
  • Larger SD = more spread out data.
  • Smaller SD = data clustered near the mean.

Plug Into the Formula

\[ s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{3 - 1}} = \sqrt{4} = 2 \]
  • The deviations \((x - \bar{x})\) always sum to 0 — a good check.
  • Square the deviations, add them, divide by \(n - 1\), then take the square root.
  • The standard deviation is \(s = 2\).

Small SD vs. Large SD

Both distributions are centered at the same mean. The standard deviation controls how spread out the data are: smaller SD → tall and narrow, larger SD → short and wide.

The 95% Rule

For approximately bell-shaped distributions, about 95% of data falls within two standard deviations of the mean.

The Rule

\[ \text{95% of data falls between } \mu - 2\sigma \text{ and } \mu + 2\sigma \]
This only applies when the distribution is roughly symmetric and bell-shaped.

Using the Rule

  • If \(\mu = 100\) and \(\sigma = 15\), then about 95% of values are between 70 and 130.
  • Values outside this range are unusual.

Z-Scores

A z-score measures how many standard deviations a value is from the mean.

Formula

\[ z = \frac{x - \text{mean}}{\text{SD}} \]
Positive z: above the mean. Negative z: below the mean.

Comparing Across Scales

Which is better: ACT of 28 or SAT of 2100?
  • ACT: \(\mu=21, \sigma=5\). \(z = (28-21)/5 = 1.4\)
  • SAT: \(\mu=1500, \sigma=325\). \(z = (2100-1500)/325 \approx 1.85\)
  • SAT score is more impressive (higher z-score).

Five Number Summary and Quartiles

Five values that capture the spread and center of a distribution.

Five Number Summary

  • Minimum: smallest value
  • Q1: 25th percentile (median of lower half)
  • Median: 50th percentile
  • Q3: 75th percentile (median of upper half)
  • Maximum: largest value

Percentiles

  • The \(P\)th percentile is the value greater than \(P\%\) of the data.
  • Q1 = 25th percentile, Median = 50th, Q3 = 75th.

Range and IQR

Two more measures of spread with different properties.

Definitions

\[ \text{Range} = \text{Max} - \text{Min} \]
\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 \]

Resistance

  • Range: not resistant (affected by outliers)
  • IQR: resistant (not affected by outliers)
  • Standard deviation: not resistant

Identifying Outliers

A rule of thumb uses the IQR to flag unusual values, and a boxplot shows them as separate points.

Outlier Rule

A data value is an outlier if it is:
\[ \text{smaller than } Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \]
\[ \text{or larger than } Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} \]
These cutoffs are the lower and upper fences.

Example

Five number summary: (2, 15, 46, 149, 536)
  • IQR = 149 - 15 = 134
  • Lower fence: 15 - 1.5(134) = -186
  • Upper fence: 149 + 1.5(134) = 350
  • Values above 350 are outliers: 402, 447, 511, 536
  • On a boxplot, these four values appear as separate dots past the upper whisker.

What Outliers Look Like

  • Each outlier is plotted individually as a dot (or asterisk) beyond the whiskers.
  • The whiskers stop at the most extreme values that are not outliers, not at the fences.
  • The fences themselves are not drawn. They only decide which values get flagged.

Determining Skewness from the Five Number Summary

Compare the distances between the five numbers. Where the median sits inside the box and which whisker is longer reveal the shape of the distribution.

Symmetric

  • The median is halfway between \(Q_1\) and \(Q_3\).
  • The distance from the minimum to the median equals the distance from the median to the maximum.
  • The left and right halves mirror each other.

Right-Skewed (Positively Skewed)

  • The median is closer to \(Q_1\) than to \(Q_3\).
  • The distance from \(Q_3\) to the maximum is much larger than the distance from the minimum to \(Q_1\).
  • The tail and the extreme high values stretch out to the right.

Left-Skewed (Negatively Skewed)

  • The median is closer to \(Q_3\) than to \(Q_1\).
  • The distance from the minimum to \(Q_1\) is much larger than the distance from \(Q_3\) to the maximum.
  • The tail and the extreme low values stretch out to the left.
Look back at the caffeine boxplot: the whisker from \(Q_3\) to the maximum is roughly twice as long as the whisker from the minimum to \(Q_1\), so that distribution is right-skewed.

What to Remember

Use mean and SD together, or use median and IQR together.

Not Resistant

  • Mean
  • Standard deviation
  • Range
Use all the data, so they carry the most information, but are affected by outliers.

Resistant

  • Median
  • IQR
Not affected by extreme values, so better when outliers are present.

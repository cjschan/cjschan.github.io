Section 5.1

Hypothesis Tests Using Normal Distributions

When the sampling distribution is approximately normal, we can replace the randomization distribution with a smooth curve and use z-scores.

The Study: Malaria and Mosquitoes

Does the malaria parasite change how mosquitoes behave before the parasite becomes infectious?

The Study

  • Researchers exposed one group of mosquitoes to the malaria parasite and left another group unexposed.
  • They then recorded whether each mosquito approached a human.
  • Is the proportion that approach a human lower for exposed mosquitoes?
\[ H_0: p_E = p_U \qquad H_a: p_E < p_U \]

The Data

GroupApproached a humanTotalProportion
Unexposed36117\(\hat{p}_U = 0.308\)
Exposed20113\(\hat{p}_E = 0.177\)
\[ \hat{p}_E - \hat{p}_U = 0.177 - 0.308 = -0.131 \]
How unusual is \(-0.131\) if \(H_0\) is true? We need a p-value.

Two Ways to Find a p-Value

Malaria and mosquitoes: \(H_0: p_E = p_U\) vs. \(H_a: p_E < p_U\), observed statistic \(= -0.131\). The p-value is the proportion of the distribution at or beyond the observed statistic.

So far (Chapter 4)

Count the dots in the tail

10 of the 1000 randomization statistics are at or below \(-0.131\), so p-value \(= 10/1000 = 0.010\).
New (Section 5.1)

Find the area in the tail

The dotplot is bell-shaped, so replace it with the smooth curve \(N(0,\ 0.056)\). The shaded area left of \(-0.131\) is the p-value \(\approx 0.0096\).
Both methods give almost the same p-value. The normal curve does not need thousands of simulated samples; it only needs the null value and the standard error.

Normal Distribution

The symmetric, bell-shaped curve that appears in nearly all sampling distributions.

Central Limit Theorem

  • For random samples with sufficiently large \(n\), the distribution of many common sample statistics is approximately normal.
  • The larger the sample size, the better the normal approximation.

Notation

\[ N(\text{mean}, \text{standard deviation}) \]
A normal distribution is fully described by its mean and standard deviation.

The Normal Curve \(N(\mu, \sigma)\)

\[ f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \]
Centered at the mean \(\mu\). The standard deviation \(\sigma\) sets the spread.

Using the Normal for Hypothesis Tests

If the randomization distribution is approximately normal, we can replace it with \(N(\text{null value}, SE)\).

From Simulation to Formula

  • Instead of simulating thousands of randomization samples, use the normal curve.
  • The p-value is the tail area beyond the observed statistic.

Example

Malaria and mosquitoes: statistic = \(-0.131\), null value = \(0\), \(SE = 0.056\)
\[ N(0,\ 0.056) \]
Compare the observed statistic to this distribution to find the p-value.

The Normal Curve in Action

A test statistic of \(z = -0.131 / 0.056 \approx -2.34\). This is a left-tailed test, so the shaded left tail is the p-value (\(\approx 0.0096\)).

The Standardized Test Statistic

Standardize to put every test on the same scale: \(N(0, 1)\).

z-Statistic

\[ z = \frac{\text{statistic} - \text{null value}}{SE} \]
This tells us how many standard errors the statistic is from the null value.

Example Calculation

\[ z = \frac{-0.131 - 0}{0.056} \approx -2.34 \]
The observed statistic is about 2.34 standard errors below the null value.

Finding the p-Value

The p-value is the tail area beyond the z-statistic in the standard normal distribution.

Process

  • Compute the z-statistic.
  • Find the area in the appropriate tail(s) of \(N(0,1)\).
  • For right-tailed: area to the right of \(z\).
  • For left-tailed: area to the left of \(z\).
  • For two-tailed: double the smaller tail.

Example

For \(z = -2.34\), left-tail:
\[ \text{p-value} \approx 0.0096 \]
Strong evidence that exposed mosquitoes are less likely to approach humans before becoming infectious.

Another Example: After Infectious

After the malaria parasite becomes infectious, do exposed mosquitoes approach humans more?

Data
Difference in proportions: \(0.151\), \(SE = 0.05\)
\[ z = \frac{0.151 - 0}{0.05} = 3.02 \]
Conclusion
\[ \text{p-value} \approx 0.0013 \]
Strong evidence that infected mosquitoes are more likely to approach humans after becoming infectious, favoring transmission of the parasite.

Your Turn: z-Tests

For each test: (i) compute the z-statistic, (ii) find the p-value (use StatKey's Normal distribution), and (iii) state a conclusion at \(\alpha = 0.05\).

(a) Right-tailed

A candidate claims majority support. In a poll, \(\hat{p} = 0.57\) with \(SE = 0.025\).

\(H_0: p = 0.5\)
\(H_a: p > 0.5\)
(b) Left-tailed

A store tests whether a new self-checkout lowers the mean checkout time below 30 seconds. A sample gives \(\bar{x} = 28.5\) seconds with \(SE = 0.6\).

\(H_0: \mu = 30\)
\(H_a: \mu < 30\)
(c) Two-tailed

Do first-year students and seniors differ in the proportion who prefer online classes? \(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2 = -0.041\) with \(SE = 0.025\).

\(H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0\)
\(H_a: p_1 - p_2 \neq 0\)
Your Turn — Answers

Answers: z-Tests

\(z = (\text{statistic} - \text{null value})/SE\), then the tail area from \(N(0,1)\).

(a) Right-tailed
\(z = \dfrac{0.57 - 0.5}{0.025} = 2.80\)

Right-tail area beyond 2.80:

p-value \(\approx 0.0026\)

\(0.0026 < 0.05\): reject \(H_0\). Strong evidence the candidate has majority support.

(b) Left-tailed
\(z = \dfrac{28.5 - 30}{0.6} = -2.50\)

Left-tail area below \(-2.50\):

p-value \(\approx 0.0062\)

\(0.0062 < 0.05\): reject \(H_0\). Strong evidence the new self-checkout lowers the mean time below 30 seconds.

(c) Two-tailed
\(z = \dfrac{-0.041 - 0}{0.025} = -1.64\)

Two tails: \(2 \times 0.0505\):

p-value \(\approx 0.10\)

\(0.10 > 0.05\): do not reject \(H_0\). Not enough evidence that the two groups differ.

What to Remember

The normal distribution provides a shortcut for computing p-values.

Formula

\[ z = \frac{\text{statistic} - \text{null value}}{SE} \]
Works when the sampling distribution is approximately normal (CLT).

Process

  • Compute the z-statistic.
  • Find the p-value from \(N(0,1)\).
  • Compare the p-value to \(\alpha\) to make a decision.
